# Glossary

::: plain

**Adaptation** — Adjustments in human and natural systems in response to actual or expected climate change, to reduce harm or exploit opportunities.

**Aerosols** — Tiny particles suspended in the air, from sources such as pollution, fires, dust and volcanoes. Many reflect sunlight and partly mask warming.

**Attribution science** — The study of how much climate change has influenced the likelihood or intensity of particular extreme events.

**Carbon budget** — The total amount of carbon dioxide that can still be emitted while keeping warming below a given level with a given probability.

**Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** — The main long-lived greenhouse gas produced by human activities, chiefly the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation.

**Carbon pricing** — Policies that put a price on greenhouse gas emissions, through a carbon tax or an emissions trading system.

**Climate** — The average weather and its variability over a long period, typically 30 years or more.

**Climate anxiety** — Distress, worry or fear related to climate change and its threats.

**Climate finance** — Funding for actions that reduce emissions or help societies adapt to climate change.

**Committed warming** — Warming that will occur because of past emissions and the slow response of the climate system, regardless of future emissions.

**Coral bleaching** — The loss of the symbiotic algae that give corals their colour and much of their food, typically caused by unusually warm water. Prolonged bleaching kills corals.

**Disaster risk reduction (DRR)** — Policies and practices aimed at preventing new disaster risk, reducing existing risk and managing residual risk.

**Early warning system** — An integrated system of hazard monitoring, forecasting, risk assessment, communication and preparedness that enables timely action.

**El Niño / La Niña** — Opposite phases of a natural climate pattern in the tropical Pacific. El Niño typically brings drought to Indonesia; La Niña brings heavier rain.

**Exposure** — The presence of people, livelihoods, assets and ecosystems in places that could be adversely affected by hazards.

**Greenhouse effect** — The warming of the Earth's surface by gases in the atmosphere that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.

**Greenhouse gas** — A gas that absorbs infrared radiation, such as carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapour.

**Hazard** — A potentially damaging physical event, such as a flood, heat wave, landslide or earthquake.

**Heat index** — A measure combining air temperature and humidity to represent how hot it feels to the human body.

**Heat stroke** — A life-threatening condition in which body temperature rises above about 40 °C and the body's cooling mechanisms fail; requires immediate cooling and medical care.

**Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)** — A natural pattern of differing sea surface temperatures between the western and eastern Indian Ocean that affects rainfall in Indonesia.

**IPCC** — The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, the United Nations body that assesses climate science.

**Land subsidence** — The sinking of the ground surface, often caused by excessive groundwater extraction and the compaction of soft soils.

**Loss and damage** — Climate impacts that cannot be avoided through mitigation or adaptation.

**Mitigation** — Actions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions or enhance their removal from the atmosphere.

**Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC)** — A country's climate action plan under the Paris Agreement, including emission targets.

**Net-zero emissions** — A state in which greenhouse gas emissions released into the atmosphere are balanced by removals.

**Paris Agreement** — The 2015 international agreement to hold warming well below 2 °C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C.

**Peatland** — Waterlogged land with thick layers of partly decomposed plant material that store large amounts of carbon; highly flammable when drained.

**Psychological first aid (PFA)** — A humane, supportive response to people suffering after a crisis, summarised as Look, Listen and Link.

**Resilience** — The capacity of people, communities and systems to anticipate, absorb, recover from and adapt to shocks and stresses.

**Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs)** — Scenarios used by the IPCC to describe alternative futures of society, emissions and climate.

**Social capital** — The networks, norms and trust that enable people to act together.

**Storm surge** — An abnormal rise of sea level during a storm, caused mainly by strong winds pushing water towards the shore.

**Tipping point** — A critical threshold beyond which a part of the climate system changes abruptly and possibly irreversibly.

**Urban heat island** — The phenomenon in which cities are warmer than surrounding rural areas because of buildings, paved surfaces and human activity.

**Vulnerability** — The propensity or predisposition of people or systems to be adversely affected by hazards.

**Wet-bulb temperature** — The lowest temperature to which air can be cooled by evaporating water; an indicator of how well sweating can cool the body.
:::
