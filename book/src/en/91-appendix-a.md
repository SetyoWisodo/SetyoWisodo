# Appendix A: Preparedness Checklists

::: plain

These checklists summarise the practical guidance in Parts III and IV. Photocopy them, adapt them to your household, and review them at least once a year — ideally before the rainy season.

## A.1 Household risk assessment

- [ ] Home address checked on InaRISK; hazard levels noted
- [ ] *Info BMKG* app installed with notifications enabled
- [ ] Past disasters in the area discussed with older neighbours
- [ ] Household members needing extra help identified
- [ ] Risk matrix completed (likelihood × impact)
- [ ] Top three priority actions chosen, with dates

## A.2 Family emergency plan

- [ ] Warning channels identified (app, radio, BPBD, mosque, siren, *kentongan*)
- [ ] Safe place in the home for each hazard
- [ ] Meeting point near home
- [ ] Evacuation destination outside the neighbourhood
- [ ] Two evacuation routes; tsunami route to high ground if coastal
- [ ] Out-of-area contact person agreed
- [ ] Responsibilities assigned (children, older relatives, pets, emergency bag)
- [ ] School and workplace emergency plans known
- [ ] Plan written down and shared with all household members
- [ ] Drill held (date: ________)

## A.3 Emergency bag (per person or per household)

- [ ] Drinking water (1–2 litres per person) and ready-to-eat food
- [ ] Copies of documents in a waterproof bag; digital copies in the cloud
- [ ] Cash in small denominations
- [ ] Flashlight or headlamp, spare batteries, power bank, cables
- [ ] Battery or hand-crank radio; whistle
- [ ] First-aid kit; two weeks of regular medicines; copies of prescriptions
- [ ] Change of clothes, raincoat, sturdy footwear, blanket or sarong
- [ ] N95 masks, soap, hand sanitiser, toothbrush, sanitary pads
- [ ] Items for infants, older people or people with disabilities
- [ ] Paper list of emergency contacts and a copy of the family plan
- [ ] Knife or multi-tool, duct tape, plastic sheet, strong bags
- [ ] Checked and refreshed (date: ________)

## A.4 Home supplies (three days minimum, two weeks in high-risk areas)

- [ ] Drinking water: 4 litres per person per day
- [ ] Water treatment: means to boil, filter or chlorinate
- [ ] Non-perishable food, rotated "first in, first out"
- [ ] Manual can opener; stove and fuel for outdoor or safe use
- [ ] Lights and batteries; solar lantern or small solar kit
- [ ] Hygiene supplies; materials for an emergency toilet
- [ ] Fire extinguisher; gas cylinder secured

## A.5 Heat-wave readiness

- [ ] Coolest room in the house identified
- [ ] Curtains or shading for sun-facing windows
- [ ] Fans working; ventilation openings unobstructed
- [ ] Oral rehydration salts and thermometer at home
- [ ] Daily check-in arranged for older or vulnerable relatives and neighbours
- [ ] Outdoor work rescheduled to cooler hours where possible
- [ ] Everyone knows the signs of heat stroke and to call 112 or 119

## A.6 Flood readiness

- [ ] Drains and gutters cleared before the rainy season
- [ ] Electrical outlets and appliances raised where possible
- [ ] High shelf or upper floor reserved for documents and supplies
- [ ] Main electricity switch location known to all adults
- [ ] Boots and gloves available for clean-up
- [ ] Evacuation trigger agreed (for example, water at a certain level or an official order)

## A.7 Financial resilience

- [ ] Separate emergency savings with regular deposits
- [ ] Some cash kept at home
- [ ] All documents scanned and stored securely
- [ ] BPJS Kesehatan active for all family members
- [ ] Property insurance reviewed for flood and earthquake coverage
- [ ] Crop or livestock insurance considered (farmers)
- [ ] Lenders checked for OJK registration

## A.8 Community

- [ ] Neighbourhood messaging group or phone tree in place
- [ ] Community risk and resource map drawn
- [ ] Confidential list of residents needing help, with assigned buddies
- [ ] Regular *kerja bakti* for drains and mosquito breeding sites
- [ ] Contact established with local BPBD, village office and *puskesmas*
- [ ] Community drill held (date: ________)
